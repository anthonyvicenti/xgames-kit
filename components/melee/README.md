# mechanic: melee

A weapon you swing (gf-scenic wave 1, track M). The creator, 2026-10-08: "Throwing snowballs at a horde is not a rogue like game similar to Lost Castle and Rampage Knights". The kit had no melee, so a sword slash became a projectile.

Add it to a spec as `{ "id": "melee", "as": "...", "params": {...} }`. Nothing to wire: `primary` swings the combo (hold it to keep swinging), `secondary` is the heavy attack, `jump` (Space) dashes. `action`, `heavyAction` and `dashAction` move them; `''` leaves an input to `rules.js` (`melee.swing(p, 'light' | 'heavy')`, `melee.dash(p)`).

## What a swing is
- **A weapon table** (`weapons`; `null` uses the kit's sword, axe and spear): a combo chain of moves, a heavy and a dash strike. A move has `windup`, `active` and `recover` (s), `arc` (°), `reach` (m), `damage`, `knockback` (m/s), `hitStop` (s), `lunge` (m/s), `sweep` (1, −1, 0 for a thrust), `tilt`, `clip`, `finisher`, `knockdown`, `guardBreak`. A weapon also has `kind`, `rarity` (0–3: +25% damage a step), `color`, and a model: `model` (a file in `assets/models/`, slot `mechanic.melee.<id>.model`) or `prop`, with its `grip` on `hand_r` (`{position, quaternion}`, as `research/gf-scenic/library/props.json` records each KayKit weapon). `mechanic.melee.weapon` (`assets/models/melee-weapon.glb`) is the start weapon's model. Without a model the kit draws a stand-in primitive in the weapon's colour slot.
- **The table is data** (open builder wave 10; `kit/assets/items.js`): `WEAPON_ITEM` says what one weapon is (each field's title, range, unit and default; the card's front; its picture, `melee-<id>.glb`, with the library's `kaykit-sword` as the default). `create` writes the engine's own table into a game whose spec names none (party: the kit's sword, axe and spear; scenic: the library's KayKit sword, axe and greatsword), so every game holds one. The whole table is the slot `mechanic.melee.weapons` (json, checked against `WEAPON_ITEM`) and the start weapon is `mechanic.melee.start-weapon`: adding, removing or renaming a weapon is one slot write (`tools/slots.mjs <game> --set`, `tools/items.mjs`). A new weapon's `model` may be `"library:<id>"`; the next `slots --write` copies it to its own file with its grip and licence. While the game runs, a weapon's numbers change live (`slotChanged`, CONTRACT §2.4); a new, removed or renamed weapon, a colour or the start weapon reload.
- **Weight**: it winds up (the body turns to the aim and walks at `moveWhileSwinging`), hits only in its active frames, lunges a step (stopping short of what stands in front), and commits the player to its recovery. A press during a swing is kept (`buffer`) and chains the next step once `chainAt` of the recovery has passed; `comboWindow` after it continues the chain.
- **Swings follow `p.aim`**: the point under the cursor with the `follow` camera, the crosshair with `third` or `first`.
- **Each target once a swing**, against the waves mechanic's enemies, the boss and (when `hitPlayers` resolves true: `auto` means not co-op) other players through fair play (§6c). Light hits push a player; finishers and heavies knock over, and fair play decides.
- **Hit-stop and shake** are this copy's own feel: the attacker's swing timeline and the drawn poses of the bodies involved hold for `hitStop`; this player's camera shakes (`feel.js`). The shared world never pauses (CONTRACT §3).
- **The dash**: `dashSpeed` for `dashTime`, invulnerable for `dashIframes` (the pose's `dg`: the host asks `dodging(p)` before an enemy's hit counts), `dashCooldown`. It cancels a windup or a recovery, never the active frames. An attack in or just after it is the dash strike. With `dashAction` "jump", the players this copy owns jump at 0 m/s.
- **What shows**: the move's clip (`slash_1`..`slash_3`, `heavy`, `dash` from track C; `punch` and `roll` on an older clip file, reported as `clips.fallback`), the weapon on `hand_r`, a slash trail (a kit primitive in `trail-color`; another player's is drawn fainter), sparks (`spark-color`, `block-color` on a guard), sounds `melee-swing`, `melee-heavy`, `melee-hit`, `melee-block`, `melee-dash` (gf-audio presets the game lists).

## Network ownership and message rates
**The copy that owns a player** (its own player; the host's bots) runs that player's swings, finds the hits on what it draws, and shows the feel at once. **The host** checks each reported hit (the target within reach + its radius + 1.6 m of where the host has the attacker; each target once a swing serial) and applies the damage from the attacker's weapon and upgrades. The pose's `extra` carries `ms` (`[swing serial, move code, yaw°]`), `md` (dash serial) and `dg` (in the dash's invulnerable frames): every other copy plays the swing, its trail and the dash from those edges. Events: `melee:hit` (a player's copy → host, once per swing that hits; at most about 4/s). Shared `melee`: `w` (weapon index per player), `r` (rarity per player), `n` (`[swings, hits]` per player, the host's count). About 30 B a player.

## Netcode traps (PLAN 2.6)
What you see is what hits: a hit is found against the bodies this copy draws, then checked by the host against where it has them, with slack for the pose's age. Remote swings and dashes play on the serial's edge. Hit-pause never pauses the net pump: hit-stop is drawing only.

## Methods for rules.js
`weaponOf(p)`, `setWeapon(p, idOrIndex, rarity)` (host), `dodging(p)`, `swing(p, kind)`, `dash(p)`, `stateOf(p)`, `shake(a)`, `onHit(fn)` (host: `{attacker, target, kind: 'enemy'|'boss'|'player', damage, move, blocked, killed}`), `onSwing(fn)`, `makeWeapon(i)`, `rarityColor(r)`, `weapons`. Upgrades come from the loot mechanic (`loot.modsOf(p)`; `UPGRADES` in `logic.js`).
